# Reading the snapshot

The snapshot is plain text with `##` sections. Its own "How to read this"
section is the authority; read it every time, because the tracker changes.
This file explains the parts that are most often misread.

## Sections you will see

| Section | What it holds | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| How to read this | The tracker's definitions for this export | Everything |
| Typical month | Coming in, fixed costs, flexible spending (average), likely left over | Slide 3, advice about spare money |
| Goals | The household's goals in priority order, with progress | Slide 6, all advice |
| Income (paychecks) | Paychecks by person and employer | Slide 1 |
| Monthly cash flow | Per month: take-home, spending, savings, debt payments, cash remaining | Slide 1 (the review month's row) |
| Spending by category | This month to date, last full month, typical month | Slide 2 |
| This month's plan | Flexible target, spent so far, available; budgets if any | Slide 3 |
| Groceries, Utilities | Detail for those two areas | Slide 4 when notable |
| Recurring obligations | Every regular bill and subscription | Slides 3, 4 |
| Savings & reserves | Funds with targets and progress | Slide 7 |
| Debts | Each debt: balance, rate, minimum, estimated interest | Slide 5 |
| Accounts and net worth | Balances, what is and is not counted | Slide 7 |
| Credit scores | Latest entered per person | Mention if changed |
| Recent monthly review notes | What the household wrote at earlier reviews | Following up on past decisions |
| Data quality notes | What is missing or waiting | Slide 8 |

A section can be absent when the tracker has nothing for it. Leave the slide
out or say the data is not there; do not fill the gap.

If two sections disagree (a count of unreviewed charges, a fund's figure
against a goal's), do not pick silently: use the figure from the section the
slide is about and note the difference under Loose ends. If a figure is
plainly unusable (a typical month showing nothing coming in while paychecks
are listed), leave it off the slides and say so under Loose ends.

## Conventions that matter

**Spending** is expenses minus refunds. Transfers between their own accounts,
savings transfers and debt payments are not spending. Paying a credit card
that is paid in full is a transfer; the purchases on it are the spending.
Do not add debt payments to spending to get "total outgo" without saying so.

**"Not categorized yet"** is real spending the household has not reviewed
yet. It counts in the totals. Mention the amount under Loose ends.

**"To date" and "this month so far"** mean the current month is not over.
If the snapshot was taken mid-month, the month's spending is partial. For a
monthly review the cleanest picture is the last full month; if the current
month is nearly complete, present it and say it is to date.

**"Provisional"** means the tracker does not have a full month of history
behind a figure yet. Carry the word onto the slide.

**Full months only.** Averages by person and the month-by-month table leave
out the month in which the records began partway through, and the month in
progress. A new tracker may therefore show no averages at all for a while.
That is expected, not an error.

**Typical month** is an average over months that have data. With one or two
months behind it, treat it as a first reading, not a baseline.

**Two meanings of "flexible".** The monthly plan's flexible target counts
spending marked Flexible. The typical-month card counts everything not marked
Fixed. The snapshot says which is which each time; keep them apart.

**Fixed costs include planned savings and debt payments** on the
typical-month card, so "likely left over" is money with no job yet. That
figure is the natural source for extra debt payments or savings.

**Funds are labels, not extra money.** A fund backed by an account is that
account's balance. Never add funds to account balances.

**Net worth is "tracked" net worth.** It counts only what is entered. A loan
with no balance entered makes it read too high. Check Data quality notes and
say so on the slide when that is the case.

**Estimated interest** is balance × rate ÷ 12 at today's balance, an
estimate. A debt with no rate entered shows no interest; that means unknown,
not zero.

**Pensions** are future income, not a balance. Do not add them to net worth.

## Months of emergency fund

The snapshot may not state this. Derive it and show the arithmetic:

    months = emergency fund ÷ (fixed costs + flexible spending − planned savings)

Use the three figures from the "Typical month" section (its flexible figure
is everything not marked Fixed). Planned savings come out because they would
pause in an emergency.
